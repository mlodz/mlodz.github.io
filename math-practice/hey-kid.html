Hey Kid,

First, let’s get an important mathematical fact out of the way: moving roughly 15,000 kilometers across the planet does weird things to your school day. Not only did the seasons flip upside down, but somewhere over the middle of the Pacific Ocean, the universe decided that Australian second-grade math would casually start asking you questions about things nobody in America had ever bothered to show you.

If someone handed you the middle of a mystery novel without letting you read chapters one through three, you wouldn’t blame yourself for not knowing who stole the emeralds. You’d just know you were missing pages. That is exactly what is happening with math right now. It isn’t a flaw in your brain; it’s just a missing chapter. And unlike stolen emeralds, missing math chapters are surprisingly easy to track down once you know where to look.

You have a mind that devours complex books and untangles intricate stories, which means you already know something most people take decades to figure out: hard things are only hard until they aren't.

Think about how a brain actually learns. It doesn't instantly possess skills like magic. When something feels confusing, frustrating, or slightly like your head is trying to digest a cactus, that isn't failure—that is literally the sound of your brain building new neuro-pathways. It’s cognitive weightlifting. The friction you feel right now is the work. And once you do the heavy lifting, the weight doesn't change, but you get stronger. The things that baffle you on Tuesday become your warm-up routine by next month.

You’ve already proven you can conquer massive, disorienting challenges. You packed up your life, flew to a continent where an army of snails seems to have quietly invaded every single footpath and people willingly eat dark, salty Vegemite by the spoonful, walked into a brand-new classroom, and found your footing. That requires an extraordinary amount of grit—far more grit, honestly, than finding a common denominator or carrying a digit.

Here is the secret about what’s happening at that school desk: you aren’t just learning how to solve equations. Numbers are just the sandbox. What you are actually learning is far bigger and infinitely cooler: you are learning how to become formidable.

You are learning what to do when you hit an invisible wall. You are learning how to stare down something unfamiliar without panicking, how to be patient with yourself when the pieces don’t immediately click, and how to build a toolkit that will let you master anything you choose to pursue later in life—whether that’s astrophysics, writing epic novels, coding software, or running an empire.

And here is the most important rule of becoming brilliant at hard things: you don't have to do it alone.

The smartest people on Earth are not the ones who sit in a corner trying to brute-force every puzzle by themselves. The smartest people are the ones who recruit allies. Letting the people who adore you—your teachers, and especially your parents—sit beside you, demystify the weird parts, and hand you the missing clues isn’t a sign of weakness. It’s strategy. It’s what great minds do.

We are going to bridge the gap between American math and Aussie math together. There will be mistakes, scribbles, probably a few dramatic sighs, and definitely some snacks involved. But every time you work through a tricky problem and refuse to surrender to it, you are laying another brick in the foundation of who you’re becoming.

So when a stubborn math problem stares back at you from the page, don’t wait for someone else to reassure you. Remind yourself to believe in your own brain. Look at the challenge and tell yourself: I haven't figured this out yet, but my mind is powerful, I can do hard things, and I have the stubbornness to solve it. Trust that brilliant head of yours, know that you’re building real power, and remember that your parents are in your corner for every single step of this adventure.

You got this.
