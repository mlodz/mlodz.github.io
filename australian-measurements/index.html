Mental Shortcuts
  • “Double and add 30” — quick C→F for everyday temps. 20°C → 40+30 = 70°F (actual 68). Good enough from 0–35°C.
  • Three anchors: 0 = freezing, 20 = room temp, 37 = body temp.
  • Each 1°C ≈ 2°F — a 5° swing in Celsius feels like 10° in Fahrenheit.
  • Baking anchor: 180°C = 350°F — the most common oven setting. Go up/down by 10°C for each ~25°F step.

Mental Shortcuts
  • Km to miles: multiply by 0.6 (or divide by 1.6). 10 km ≈ 6 miles.
  • Cm to feet: 30 cm ≈ 1 foot. For height, 152 cm = 5′0″ then add ~2.5 cm per inch.
  • Km/h to mph: drop 40%. 100 km/h → 60 mph.

Mental Shortcuts — Weight
  • Kg to lbs: double it, then add 10%. 70 kg → 140 + 14 = 154 lbs (exact: 154.3).
  • Lbs to kg: halve it, then subtract 10%. 180 lbs → 90 - 9 = 81 kg (exact: 81.6).
  • Quick anchor: 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lbs. Just over double.
  • At the butcher/grocery: 500 g ≈ 1 lb. If you’d buy “a pound of ground beef,” ask for 500 grams.

Mental Shortcuts — Volume
  • Litres to gallons: roughly 4 litres per gallon. A 60 L fuel tank ≈ 16 gallons.
  • mL to fl oz: divide by 30. 250 mL ÷ 30 ≈ 8 oz.
  • Aus cup = 250 mL, slightly bigger than a US cup (237 mL). Close enough for most recipes.
  • At the pub: a schooner (425 mL) is a bit less than a US pint (473 mL). An Aus pint (570 mL) is bigger than a US pint.

How Australians Actually Say It

Units get shortened and dropped in everyday speech. You’ll be understood using the full word, but you’ll sound textbook-formal.

You’d say (US)They say (Aus)Example
“75 kilograms”“75 kilos”“I weigh about 80 kilos”
“10 kilometers”“10 kays” (or “10 k’s”)“It’s about 10 kays from here”
“100 kilometers per hour”“100 kays an hour”“I was doing 110 kays an hour”
“35 degrees Celsius”“35 degrees”“It’s gonna be 35 degrees tomorrow”
“180 centimeters”“180” (no unit)“Yeah, he’s about 180”
“10 millimeters”“10 mil”“Hand me the 10 mil spanner”
“250 millilitres”“250 mils”“Add 250 mils of stock”
“litres”“litres” (no shortening)“Put 40 litres in”
The Pattern
  • Australians shorten everything they can: kilos, kays, mils.
  • Units get dropped when context makes them obvious — “I’m 180” for height, “it’s 35 degrees” for weather, “doing 110” for driving speed.
  • Celsius, centimeters, and km/h are always assumed. You never need to specify the unit — metric is the default.
