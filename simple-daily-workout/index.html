⚡ The Simple Daily Workout

🧠 Stupidly-Simple Memory Cheat Sheet

If your brain is totally dead, just remember: RUN – 3 SUPERSETS – RUN

  1. RUN to park
  2. VERTICAL (Dips + Pull-ups)
  3. HORIZONTAL (Push-ups + Rows)
  4. LOWER & CORE (Squats + Knee Raises)
  5. RUN home

Action creates motivation, not the other way around. Just lace up and step outside.

🎯 Goals & Timeline

Based on 2–3 sessions per week, lean bodyweight (about 152 lb), age 43. Three sessions a week gets the faster end of each range.

TimeframeWhat you are building
1 Month4–6 unassisted dips · 5–8 strict push-ups (incline sets of 15–20) · 3–5 slow pull-up negatives, maybe your first pull-up
3 Months8–10 unassisted dips · 12–18 strict push-ups · 2–3 pull-ups
6 Months15+ unassisted dips · 25–30 push-ups · 5–6 pull-ups
Finish lines12 dips: ~4 months · 40 push-ups: ~8–10 months · 10 pull-ups: ~10–14 months

🏃 Step 1: Outbound

  • Run 1.4 km to the outdoor gym (more if you feel good, but keep it conversational).
  • Mindset: Don't overthink. Just put one foot in front of the other.
  • Warm up 5 minutes: arm circles, scapular pull-ups, a few easy push-ups.

🏋️ Step 2: The Park Workout

Rule: Alternate Exercise A and B. Rest 2–3 minutes after hard sets on pull-ups, dips and push-ups. 60–90 seconds is fine for easier sets.

🔴 Superset 1: Vertical (Dips + Pull-Ups)

  1. 3 sets of Dips (less foot assistance each week)
  2. 3 sets of Pull-Ups (negatives, or chin-ups first)

Pattern: Dips → Rest → Pull-Ups → Rest → Repeat 3×

🔵 Superset 2: Horizontal (Push-Ups + Rows)

  1. 3 sets of Push-Ups (incline, lowered over time)
  2. 3 sets of Inverted Rows (feet on ground, low bar)

Pattern: Push-Ups → Rest → Rows → Rest → Repeat 3×

🟢 Superset 3: Lower & Core (Squats + Hanging Knee Raises)

  1. 3 sets of Single-Leg Squats (8–10 per leg, rear knee drops to ground)
  2. 3 sets of Hanging Knee Raises (8–12 reps, smooth and controlled)

Pattern: Squats → Rest → Knee Raises → Rest → Repeat 3×

🏠 Step 3: Inbound

  • Run 1.4 km back home.

📈 How to Progress

The core rule: pick a variation where you can do 8–12 reps per set with 1–2 reps left in the tank. Use the same variation each session and add a rep when you can. When you hit 12 reps on all 3 sets, move to the next harder variation. Reps will drop to about 6–8, then you build back up.
Don't do sets of 40 or train to failure every set.
Push-ups
  1. Steep incline (waist-height bar or higher) until 3×12.
  2. Lower the incline in steps (knee height, then bench height).
  3. Flat push-ups as slow negatives: lower over 3–5 seconds, drop to knees, push back up. Aim for 3 sets of 3–5.
  4. With 5+ strict flat push-ups, do 3 sets of 5–8, then build toward 10, 12, and on to 40.

Add one slow negative set each session at your hardest level.

Pull-ups and rows

Rows come first because they build pulling strength.

  • Make rows harder: feet farther forward, then feet elevated, then a 3-second lowering, then a pause at the top. Aim for 3×10 before progressing.
  • Negatives: jump to the top position and lower over 5 seconds. 3 sets of 3–5 is the fastest route to your first pull-up.
  • Chin-ups first: underhand grip is easier. Try both.
  • Once you can do 1–2, do sets at about half your max (max of 4 means sets of 2).
Dips
  • Reduce foot pressure each week: toes only, then one foot, then hovering.
  • Add slow 3-second negatives.
  • At 5–8 unassisted, build to 12 across 3 sets, then add a 2-second pause at the bottom.
Single-leg squats
  • Make them harder before adding reps: 3-second lowering, then a pause at the bottom.
  • Then move the back foot onto a bench (Bulgarian split squat).
  • Later, try a pistol squat to a bench.

When it gets too easy (no weights)

  1. Slower tempo (3–5 second lowering)
  2. Pauses at the hardest point
  3. Harder leverage (lower incline, elevated feet, one-leg or archer variations)
  4. Rest changes: shorter for endurance, 2–3 minutes for strength

Later, a backpack with water bottles can add weight.

🛡️ Recovery & Age Notes

  • Muscles adapt fine at 43. Tendons and joints (elbows, shoulders, wrists) lag, so protect them.
  • Train 2–3 days a week with at least one rest day between sessions. Extra rest is better than pushing through cranky elbows or shoulders.
  • Stop dips on sharp pain at the front of the shoulder. Keep your chest slightly forward and don't go deeper than about 90° at the elbow.
  • Sleep well and eat about 0.7–1 g protein per lb of bodyweight (roughly 110–150 g a day).
  • Test your max reps once a month, not every session.
  • If you gain muscle and reach about 160 lb, pull-ups get slightly harder per rep, so timelines can shift a little.

👀 How You'll Look

Expect a change in shape more than size: fit and athletic (gymnast or climber), not bulky.

WhenWhat changes
1–3 MonthsMostly strength and skill gains, little visible change. Shoulders and upper back may look a bit firmer, and posture improves.
3–6 MonthsFuller upper chest, more defined shoulders and triceps, the start of a V-taper from rows and pull-ups, stronger-looking forearms. Others begin to notice, especially in a fitted t-shirt.
9–14 MonthsWith the pull-up and push-up targets reached: a broad-looking back, defined arms and a solid chest.

What other people notice

  • Broader-looking shoulders and back in a t-shirt
  • Better posture (pulling work counters desk hunching)
  • Harder, more defined arms rather than bigger ones
  • Your endurance from the 2.8 km run

Realistic limits

  • Without added weight, muscle size plateaus sooner than in a gym program. Expect a few pounds of muscle over a year, some of it showing up as strength.
  • Legs get stronger but won't grow much, and running mostly builds endurance.
  • Visible definition (abs, veins) depends mostly on body fat and diet.
  • Genetics affect where and how fast you gain muscle.
  • To speed visible change: eat enough protein, don't stay in a calorie deficit, sleep 7–8 hours, and be consistent for months.

💚 How You'll Feel

After 1 month
  • Physically: The first two weeks are hardest, with soreness in chest, lats and triceps and possibly cranky elbows or shoulders. By weeks 3–4 it fades and exercises start to feel manageable.
  • Energy and sleep: Often deeper sleep and steadier energy, though some feel more tired at first.
  • Mood: A small, reliable lift on training days from the exercise and time outdoors.
  • Mentally: Showing up starts to feel automatic, though skipped weeks can still derail it.
After 6 months
  • Physically: Carrying things, climbing stairs and lifting the kids or luggage feel easier. Less stiffness, better posture, faster recovery.
  • Confidence: Often the biggest change. Going from almost one push-up to 20–30 proves steady effort works.
  • Mood and stress: Regular exercise is one of the better-supported ways to manage stress and improve mood, though it isn't a substitute for other support if you need it.
  • Identity: It becomes part of who you are, so a missed week feels off rather than like failure.

Along the way

  • Plateaus: 2–3 weeks of no progress is normal, especially on pull-ups. A rest week or a small variation change usually breaks it.
  • Motivation dips: Around weeks 6–10 the novelty fades. This is when the 60% effort mindset matters most.
  • Uneven progress: Dips and push-ups will improve faster than pull-ups. Track your own progress.

A mediocre 60% effort workout builds 100% of the habit. You never regret the workout you finished.
